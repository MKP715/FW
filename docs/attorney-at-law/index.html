Questions

Before you call.

Do I really need a lawyer?

You never need a lawyer for anything, if you are willing, able, and have the requisite skill to handle a matter yourself. The operative words are “requisite skill.” If you perform a task incorrectly, you pay a lot more than had you invested in a lawyer.

The same goes for a trademark search: it isn’t required, but if you bypass this vital step, you do so at your peril.

Is the first consultation really complimentary?

Yes. All appointments are virtual, and initial consultations are complimentary, by appointment. You can book a 30-minute call online or send a message.

Can you review a contract I’ve already been sent?

Yes. Publishing, freelance, collaboration and business agreements can be reviewed before you sign — with an eye to the rights you’re granting, how and when you get paid, and whether your rights ever come back to you.

When should I register a copyright?

Copyright exists once your work is fixed in a tangible form, but registration is generally required before you can sue for infringement of a U.S. work — and registering before infringement, or within three months of publication, preserves statutory damages and attorney’s fees.

What does an estate plan cost?

It depends on the type of plan, the complexity of your estate, how many properties are involved, and whether the plan is for one person or a couple. As Francine explains: “With my clients, I usually include in my flat fee the primary residence. I prepare the deeds and the supplemental recording documents required by the county.”
