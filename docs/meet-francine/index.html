Meet Francine

It’s never too lateto keep on trying.

Attorney, author and speaker Francine D. Ward has spent 35 years helping people protect their families, their legacies and the businesses they’ve built. Her own story — from the streets of New York to Georgetown Law — is the proof behind everything she teaches.

Hello, I’m Francine

I built my practice to prevent those moments.

Most people don’t realize how exposed they are until something forces them to find out. An outdated will that no longer reflects their life. A brand name they assumed was “theirs” until someone else claimed it first. A family left guessing because no one ever talked about what should happen.

These are the moments that create chaos, conflict, and unnecessary pain — and they’re almost always preventable. With 35 years of experience as an attorney and a J.D. from Georgetown University Law Center, I’ve spent my career helping people protect what matters most: their families, their legacies, and the identities of the businesses they’ve worked hard to build.

How I work

Truth, transparency and thoughtful guidance.

My approach is intentionally different. I listen deeply. I ask the questions most people avoid. I help you see the bigger picture — not just what documents you need, but why you need them, how they work, and how they protect you in real life.

Clients come to me because they want more than paperwork. They want a lawyer who:

  • tells the truth with compassion,
  • understands the complexity of family dynamics,
  • respects the emotional weight of planning for the future,
  • and can translate complicated issues into clear, grounded decisions.

My work blends legal strategy with clarity coaching and honest conversation. Estate planning and trademark protection aren’t just legal tasks; they’re acts of responsibility, love, and foresight. My goal is simple: to help you make decisions today that stand the test of time.

The journey

From the streets of New York to Georgetown Law.

It took Francine years to tell her story openly and without shame. Now she shares it on stage, in her books and with clients, because hearing it “helps them know anything can be overcome.”

Read her story in her words →
  1. Age 14

    Addicted at fourteen

    At fourteen, Francine was a heroin addict and a practicing alcoholic.

  2. Age 18

    Homeless in New York

    At eighteen, she was a high school dropout living homeless on the streets of New York.

  3. Age 26

    Told she’d never walk again

    At twenty-six, she was hit by a car and told she would never walk again.

  4. Recovery

    Getting clean and sober

    Francine got clean and sober, and stayed that way. “I recognized that I couldn’t change without help—lots of help,” she writes. “I asked for help and took the suggestions given.”

  5. 1989

    Georgetown University Law Center

    The high school dropout earned her J.D. from Georgetown University Law Center. She is admitted to practice in California, New York and the District of Columbia.

  6. 1995

    Finding her voice on stage

    Francine began speaking professionally, engaging audiences large and small with her story of triumph over seemingly insurmountable odds.

  7. Early 40s

    Two marathons

    The woman who was told she’d never walk again ran two 26.2-mile marathons in her early forties.

  8. 2003 & 2005

    Esteemable Acts®

    Her approach became a book: Esteemable Acts: 10 Actions for Building Real Self-Esteem (Broadway Books, 2003), followed by 52 Weeks of Esteemable Acts: A Guide to Right Living (Hazelden, 2005) and Staying Legal (ASTD Press) in 2007. See the books →

  9. 2021

    WealthCounsel Trailblazer Award

    Honored by WealthCounsel for the impact she made on its community of attorneys. Her takeaway: “it’s never too late to keep on trying.”

  10. Today

    A life of service

    Forty-three years clean and sober as of 2022, Francine practices intellectual property and estate planning law from Palm Desert, California, writes the Esteemable Acts journal, and works out almost daily. Her life, she says, “is about being of service to others.”

Every age, a new chapter

Life is not a destination, it’s a journey.

As the years have passed, Francine’s look has changed, her thinking has changed, and her life condition has changed. What has not changed is her message of hope. In her own words:

  1. 18

    At 18, I thought I was invincible.

  2. 25

    Yet at 25, I didn’t think I’d live to see 26.

  3. 30

    By the time I turned 30, I was in the middle of a significant life change.

  4. 40

    Then by 40, I was living my dream.

  5. 50

    At 50, I couldn’t believe how awesome I felt. 50 rocked!

  6. 60

    Then I hit 60 and I felt my life was over. I cried for days and weeks at the thought of being old. I was probably watching way too many television shows, where 60-year-old people were in rocking chairs.

  7. 61

    By 61, I started to appreciate the gift of a long life.

  8. 71

    Now at 71, I am living and loving every fabulous moment of it.

“The true test of a successful person is their willingness to weather life’s ‘inevitable’ storms. Everyone has their turn in the barrel. Aging is one of life’s storms. Loss is one of life’s storms. … If we are lucky, we GET to experience all with room to spare. I speak from experience.”
