Esteemable Acts

Self-esteem comes fromdoing esteemable acts.

Real self-esteem doesn't come from quick fixes or slogans — it comes from action. It isn't something you acquire. It's something you practice into being.

Explore the books →Free worksheets

Self-esteem comes from doing esteemable acts — those things that make you feel good about yourself. Walking through fear, making right choices even when it's really hard to do, and keeping agreements with yourself.

— Francine D. Ward

The philosophy

Five principles that change everything.

Most self-help asks you to think differently and hope you'll act differently. Esteemable Acts flips it: change what you do, and the way you see yourself follows.

1

Self-esteem is not a quick fix.

It challenges the myth that all you need to do is stand in front of a mirror, chant affirmations, or complete a 7, 30, or 60-day program.

2

Self-esteem isn't confidence.

Plenty of accomplished people still mistreat others or need to put others down to feel worthy. Confidence is about ability; self-esteem is about how you live.

3

It's an outcome, not the goal.

Self-esteem is a byproduct of a courageous, purposeful, service-driven life — what happens to you along the way.

4

Esteemable acts build it.

Behavior comes first. When your behavior reflects your values, you naturally feel better about yourself and more mindful of how you treat others.

5

Courage grows it.

Every time you face a fear or finish something you doubted you could do, your self-esteem gets stronger.

Practice

Ten esteemable acts to start with this week.

Drawn from the themes of Francine's books and journal. Pick one. Do it today. Notice how you feel tonight. That's the whole method.

  1. Take action

    Action is the key. Feelings follow behavior — so start by doing, not by waiting to feel ready.

  2. Walk through fear

    Fear isn't a stop sign. Do the thing that scares you, and notice who you become on the other side.

  3. Make the right choice

    Even when it's really hard to do. Especially then.

  4. Keep agreements with yourself

    The promises you make to yourself are the ones that build — or break — your self-respect.

  5. Live on purpose

    Know your values, and let your calendar and your checkbook reflect them.

  6. Take baby steps

    Small, consistent actions beat dramatic overhauls. Consistency isn't perfection — it's resilience.

  7. Let a mentor in

    None of us gets there alone. Mentors are a gift: let someone who has walked the road show you the way.

  8. Forgive

    Forgiveness isn't excusing harm. It's the gift you give to yourself so you can move forward.

  9. Be of service

    Helping others is one of the fastest ways to feel good about yourself — and to remember you matter.

  10. Practice gratitude — and have fun

    Gratitude is a muscle, and choosing joy, on occasion, is an esteemable act too.
