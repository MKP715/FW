Meet the speaker

Her story is the message.

At fourteen, Francine was a heroin addict and a practicing alcoholic. At eighteen, she was a high-school dropout living homeless on the streets of New York. At twenty-six, she was hit by a car and told she’d never walk again.

She went on to graduate from Georgetown University Law Center, practice law for 35 years, run two marathons in her early forties, and write two traditionally published self-help books. In an engaging, intelligent and often humorous style, she shares more than her story. She shows audiences that anything is possible, explains the Esteemable Acts® approach to lasting self-esteem, and makes the case for the unfashionable basics: hard work and determination.

Featured keynote

Fear Ain’t Nothing But A Four‑Letter Word

  • Keynote
  • Motivation & inspiration

Francine on walking through fear →

What is more cunning, baffling, powerful, and more deadly than a speeding bullet? FEAR!

It ruins families and … is the single biggest barrier to living a life of endless possibilities. F-E-A-R keeps us stuck, broke, dependent, and living beyond our means. It is the ultimate jailor because when F-E-A-R comes to visit it takes plenty of prisoners.

It is the one thing we all have in common, whether we are head of our own company, working for someone else, retired, single, married, divorced, or in search of ourselves. In this program Francine demonstrates strategies for helping the audience get past this deadly killer of hope and dreams.

Self-Esteem Comes from DOING Esteemable Acts

Real self-esteem doesn’t come from chanting affirmations in a mirror or finishing a 30-day program. It comes from action — consistent, often uncomfortable steps that line up with the person you want to be. In the words of the Esteemable Acts approach: “You can’t think your way into right living—you must act your way into right thinking.”

Long bio

Francine D. Ward is proof that change is possible. At fourteen, she was a heroin addict and a practicing alcoholic. At eighteen, she was a high-school dropout living homeless on the streets of New York. At twenty-six, she was hit by a car and told she’d never walk again; in her early forties, she ran two marathons. She has been clean and sober for more than four decades.

Francine graduated from Georgetown University Law Center in 1989 and has 35 years of experience as an attorney. Admitted in California, New York and the District of Columbia, she helps clients protect their brands and creative work through trademarks and copyrights, and protect their families through wills, trusts and estate plans. She has taught intellectual property and contract drafting at the California Desert Trial Academy of Law in Indio, California, has served on the governing council of the American Bar Association’s Intellectual Property Law Section and chaired its Attorney Wellness Committee, and received the WealthCounsel Trailblazer Award in 2021.

She is the author of Esteemable Acts: 10 Actions for Building Real Self-Esteem (Broadway Books, 2003) and 52 Weeks of Esteemable Acts: A Guide to Right Living (Hazelden, 2005), and of Staying Legal: A Guide to Copyright and Trademark Use (ASTD Press). A professional speaker since 1995, she speaks on fear, self-esteem, recovery and the legal landmines of social media. Her law office is in Palm Desert, California.
