How much does it cost?

The cost of estate planning varies because every person and situation is different. No two individuals are the same and no two families have the same goals. There are many factors that go into how much an attorney charges for a comprehensive estate plan. This blog post will examine some of those factors.

What Determines the Cost of an Estate Plan?

1. Type of Plan: Trust‑based vs. Will‑based

  • Trust‑based plans generally cost more because they include a Living Trust, funding guidance, and additional documents that help avoid probate.
  • Will‑based plans are typically less expensive upfront but may lead to higher costs later due to probate.

2. Attorney’s Fee Structure

  • Flat‑fee attorneys provide predictable pricing and clear scope.
  • Hourly attorneys bill for time spent, which can increase costs if the estate is complex or requires multiple revisions. Often, everything is included in the billable hour calculation e.g., drafting emails, phones, reviewing documents, revisions, and more.

3. Complexity of the Estate

  • Simple estates with one home, straightforward beneficiaries, and no business interests cost less.
  • Complex estates—multiple properties, blended families, business ownership, tax planning—require more drafting and coordination.

4. Number of Properties

Real property is one of the biggest cost variables.

  • Primary residence included. Some lawyers include funding the primary residence into the trust as part of the initial flat fee.
  • Additional properties. Additional properties will almost always require an additional cost, especially if they are located outside of residential state or out of the country. If the attorney is not admitted to practice I that secondary jurisdiction, they often are asked to help the client secure a local attorney.

5. Whether the Plan Is for One Person or a Couple

  • Individual plans require one set of documents.
  • Couple’s plans require coordinated documents for two people, often with mirrored provisions, but not always.

6. Attorney Experience and Community

  • Attorneys with decades of experience, specialized training, or a focus on estate planning typically charge more.
  • Geographic location also affects pricing—urban areas and high‑cost communities tend to have higher fees.

What’s Included in a Comprehensive Estate Plan?

A complete plan generally includes:

  • Revocable Living Trust (if trust‑based)
  • Last Will and Testament
  • Assignment of Assets
  • Certificate of Trust
  • Durable Power of Attorney
  • Advance Healthcare Directive
  • HIPAA Authorization
  • Trust funding guidance
  • Property deeds (primary residence included; additional properties extra)
  • And sometimes more

Why Flat‑Fee Pricing Works Well for Clients

Flat‑fee pricing provides:

  • Predictability — no surprise bills
  • Clarity — clients know exactly what’s included
  • Efficiency — encourages thoughtful conversations without worrying about hourly charges
  • Transparency — clients understand the value of each component

With my clients, I usually include in my flat fee the primary residence. I prepare the deeds and the supplemental recording documents required by the county. This is important for my clients, because they want their home to be in the trust.

Why Costs Vary Between Attorneys

Estate planning is not a commodity. Costs vary because:

  • Some attorneys offer bare‑bones documents, while others provide comprehensive counseling.
  • Some include funding, property transfers, or ongoing support; others do not.
  • Experience matters—an attorney with 30+ years of practice brings a different level of insight and risk‑prevention than a new lawyer.
  • Community cost of living affects overhead and pricing.

Estate Planning is a commodity when dealing with trust mills

What’s a trust mill? It’s a non‑attorney sales organization masquerading as a law firm. These companies sell low‑cost, generic trust documents—often at seminars, hotel luncheons, or “free dinner” events—while upselling high‑commission annuities or insurance products.

In California, they frequently violate the California Business & Professions Code § 6125, which prohibits the unauthorized practice of law. Non‑attorneys cannot draft legal documents, give legal advice, or present themselves as estate planning experts. Be ware!

The Bottom Line

Estate planning is an investment in clarity, protection, and peace of mind. While costs vary, what matters most is choosing an attorney who listens, understands your goals, and provides a plan tailored to your unique circumstances, and who understands the law. A well‑crafted estate plan saves your loved ones time, money, and stress—and ensures your wishes are honored.
