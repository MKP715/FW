A HIPAA Authorization is one document included in complete estate plan. In 1996, Congress enacted the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) to establish national standards for protecting patient privacy. While most people associate HIPAA with hospitals refusing to share information, its purpose is far broader: to ensure that every adult’s medical information is handled with consistency, confidentiality, and respect.

Once a person turns 18, they are legally considered an adult. That means their medical information is protected from disclosure — even to parents, guardians, adult children, siblings, spouses, teachers, or coaches. Without proper authorization, even the people you trust most may be barred from receiving updates or making decisions on your behalf.

HIPAA Authorization. When there is None.

HIPAA’s protections are essential, but they can create serious challenges when someone becomes ill or incapacitated and has not executed a valid HIPAA Authorization or Healthcare Directive. Without these documents:

  • No one can legally make medical decisions for the incapacitated person during an emergency.
  • Family members cannot access medical records or speak with doctors without obtaining a court order — a process that is often slow and expensive.
  • Critical, time‑sensitive decisions may be delayed, especially in life‑or‑death situations.
  • Healthcare providers may refuse to release information out of fear of violating federal law, even when family members are desperate for answers.

These barriers can cause confusion, conflict, and unnecessary suffering at a time when clarity and compassion are needed most.

HIPAA Authorization. WHY IT Matters?

A valid HIPAA Authorization allows you to designate the individuals who may access your medical information and communicate with your healthcare providers. When paired with a Healthcare Directive, it ensures:

  • Your chosen decision‑makers can act quickly.
  • Your medical team can share information freely with the people you trust.
  • Your wishes are honored without court involvement.
  • Your family avoids unnecessary stress, conflict, and legal hurdles.

How to Create a HIPAA Authorization

If your HIPAA Authorization is part of a comprehensive estate plan, your attorney will ensure that all documents — including your Will, Trust, Healthcare Directive, and Power of Attorney — work together seamlessly.

If created as a standalone document (which is not ideal), you may use your state’s approved forms or have an attorney prepare one for you. Each state maintains its own repository of medical forms.

For California residents, forms are available through the California Department of Health Care Services.

Final Thoughts

HIPAA protections are essential, but without proper planning, they can unintentionally create barriers during medical emergencies. A thoughtfully prepared HIPAA Authorization — ideally integrated into your full estate plan — ensures that the people you trust most can support you when it matters most. Estate planning is not only about distributing assets; it is about protecting your autonomy, your dignity, and your loved ones.
