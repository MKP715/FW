Artificial Intelligence (AI) is transforming nearly every area of our lives, and estate planning is no exception. AI provides powerful tools for gathering, organizing, and managing information, but it cannot replace the strategic judgment and legal expertise provided by an experienced and competent estate planning attorney. Even lawyers who rely on AI without verifying information have found courts unsympathetic to their unvetted approach. If trained professionals can get it wrong, imagine the risks for individuals attempting to create legal documents without guidance.

AI and Estate Planning - Trust vs. Will

Whether you need a trust‑based estate plan or a will‑based estate plan depends on the type of assets you own, the size of your estate, and the state where your property is located. Understanding the nuances of both structures is essential. AI may offer general information, but it cannot analyze your specific situation or apply state‑specific laws.

AI Helps You Get Organized — But It Doesn’t Create a Legal Plan.
AI can help you inventory accounts, track digital property, identify missing documents, and prepare questions for your attorney. But AI cannot create a legally enforceable estate plan. Only a licensed attorney can ensure your documents comply with California law and accurately reflect your wishes. Details such as naming a trust, granting trustee powers, and applying state law require human judgment.

AI and Estate Planning - Smart Software.

Software may be smart, but it is only as smart as the human who inputs the information. Prudence is not a quality ascribed to computer software. I routinely test AI systems by asking questions I already know the answers to or can easily verify. Often these are questions I consider simple, and AI still gets them wrong. Sometimes I’ll even ask, “Are you sure?” and the software confidently repeats the same incorrect answer. If AI struggles with basic factual questions, it certainly cannot navigate the nuance, judgment, and state‑specific requirements often involved in estate planning.

AI and Estate Planning - Digital Assets.

Your estate now includes email accounts, social media profiles, cloud storage, cryptocurrency, NFTs, online business assets, and digital intellectual property. AI tools can help track these assets, but they must be intentionally included in your estate plan.

AI and Smart Wills AI and blockchain technologies are creating “smart wills” that automatically execute certain instructions. These tools are promising but still evolving and must be integrated carefully with traditional estate planning.

AI and Long‑Term Planning AI can model tax scenarios, market fluctuations, and long‑term estate projections. These tools support your attorney’s strategy but do not replace it.

The Risk of DIY AI Estate Planning AI‑generated documents often contain errors, invalid signatures, missing trust funding, conflicting instructions, or provisions that violate California law. AI can help you prepare, but only an attorney can protect you.
